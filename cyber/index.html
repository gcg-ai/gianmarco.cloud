Cybersecurity Law & Policy in Mexico

Mexico polices cybercrime without a cybersecurity law, and it is one of only two countries in this study that never joined the Budapest Convention — the treaty that makes cross-border evidence move. That gap is not abstract. It is why an investigation stalls while the evidence sits on a server abroad. The other five closed the same gap in very different ways: one concentrated all the authority, one scattered it, one moved only after an attack left it no choice.

So I mapped them against Mexico — the United States, the EU with Germany, Brazil, Colombia, Singapore and Estonia — six countries on six dimensions, every claim tied to a primary source. Spin the globe, open a country, and decide for yourself which models Mexico should copy, adapt, or refuse.
